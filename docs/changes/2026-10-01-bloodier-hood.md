# Bloodier bonnets, and the first stain no longer hitches
- A fatal hit at speed now leaves the whole front of the car bloody: a heavy impact mass and the pad where the body slid, broad wipe fingers, a band of streaks and a few thick beaded ropes dragged back along the airflow from the bumper over the bonnet, up the windscreen and onto the roof edge, with feathered hairlines, a thrown spray of drops and mist, darker crimson wet and brown-black dry. A survivor's stain stays small and short, a slow bump leaves nothing.
- The streaks follow the car: they grow back along the bonnet while it keeps moving (about a second at 60 km/h) and stop wherever it stopped; the slow gravity runs on the bonnet and down the bumper only creep once the car has slowed. A stain that is out of view carries on the same way.
- No hitch: the fit and the paint run in time slices (about 3 ms a frame, up to 9 only when the frame is already slow), the body's surfaces are cached per vehicle type, the canvas, texture and shader program are made and linked in advance while the title menu is up, and a painted tile is uploaded as a sub-image. On a headless software renderer the first stain used to cost one 890-1,060 ms frame (the shader compile at first draw) plus 60-85 ms (fit) and 30-55 ms (paint) frames; now it is about 30 slices of at most a few ms and no frame stands out.
- Internals: stain records gain `reach`, `flow`, `creep` (`updateCarStainFlow`, car-stains.js: flow advances above 4 m/s only, creep once the car is slow); the sheet's G and B hold arrival values times thickness and the shader reveals them with each stain's `flow` and `creep` (uniform `uBloodEv`); carblood3d-streaks.js (new) holds the strands, carblood3d-skin.js (new) the material, skin pool, frame budget (`cbSlice`), warm-up and tile upload; fatal severity is 0.62-1.
- Console: `carBloodReport(reset)` also reports `reach`, `flow`, `creep` and the skin's cost (slices, uploads, worst frame, frame gaps); `carBloodMark(..., flow)`. Test: tools/tests/car-blood.mjs checks reach, flow and creep.
