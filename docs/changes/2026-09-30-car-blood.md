# Blood stains on cars that run people over
- A car that kills or badly hurts someone now carries a real stain: an impact mass with fingers, a spray of round and drawn-out drops and fine mist thrown back along the airflow (up the windscreen), a smear that lengthens with speed, and on the bumper and grille runs that creep down. It follows the real bodywork of every vehicle (bonnet, nose, fender, door side, windscreen base), including a crumpled or buckled one, and it is lit like the paint, day and night.
- Where the person was struck decides where it goes (nose, tail or a flank); a fatal or fast hit leaves a proper splatter, a survivor's a small one, a slow bump nothing. Up to three stains a car.
- Blood is glossy crimson while wet, dries to matte brown-black over about two minutes, fades only slowly, and is gone after a respray or repair, or a minute or so of heavy rain. The 2D view shows a dark splat where the body was struck.
- Internals: `c.stains` records (car-stains.js); carblood3d.js clips decal geometry to the model's meshes (one mesh, one material, one canvas per stained car, five cars at most), the stain is a thickness field painted once; replaces the nine red boxes and `c.bloodyUntil`.
- Console: `carBloodReport()`, `carBloodVictim(along, lateral)`, `carBloodMark(face, kph, fatal, across, age)`, `bloodEnabled(on)`. Test: tools/tests/car-blood.mjs.
