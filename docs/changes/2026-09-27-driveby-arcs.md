# Drive-bys: real firing arcs and a leaning-out arm
- Shooting from a vehicle follows the body and the glass: the driver (left-hand drive) fires out of their own window from 25 to 155 degrees off the nose, across the passenger seat out of the far window from 52 to 125, and, turned round, back through the rear screen of cars that have one (the first shot bursts it). Nothing goes forward through your own windscreen (until it is broken); box trucks, the bus, the ambulance, panel vans, flatbeds, police cars (the cage) and mid-engine supercars cannot shoot straight back. Riders shoot left-handed, never back through themselves; roadsters and boats are open.
- An aim just past an arc's edge fires along the edge; further out the gun holds fire, a hint says why and the new drive-by reticle (a line of fire from the muzzle and a ring) dims; amber when clamped.
- The pose: the window winds down, the pistol comes up from the lap and out over the sill in a quarter second, the arm reaching it from the shoulder by IK (elbow bent, wrist on the aim), the torso leaning to the window and turning with the aim, the head along it; the free hand stays on the wheel and turns with the steering. Each shot kicks the muzzle up and back; the flash is at the muzzle out of the window. On a bike the left hand leaves the bar.
- Keyboard aim from a vehicle picks a threat inside the arcs, else fires straight out of the driver's window.
- Internals: `spec.driveBy` on every vehicle definition (driveby.js `driveByDefaults`), `driveByAim` (the arcs and clamp), `driveByGrip` (the muzzle both the bullet and the pose use), `c.windowsDown`; crowd3d-driveby.js; `city3D.fire(..., height)`.
- Console: `driveBy()`, `driveByArcs(type)`, `driveByCheck(relDegrees)`, `driveByAim(relDegrees, fire)`. Test: tools/tests/driveby-arcs.mjs.
