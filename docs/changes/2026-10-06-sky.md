# Sky, clouds and haze from street level
- Chase view: a new sky. A deeper blue zenith over a bright hazy horizon band, warm scattered light round the sun that spreads gold along the horizon at dawn and dusk, a limb-darkened sun disc bright enough to bloom; at night stars, the moon with its halo and an orange-brown glow of city light low over the horizon; grey and flat under an overcast.
- The sun in the sky now really sets (its light still stops ~15 degrees up for readable shadows); in the chase view the sea's glitter lies under it.
- Clouds from below: HIGH and ULTRA march up into the real cloud layer (the one that throws the shadows), lit by the sun, gold at dusk, grey under a deck, lit orange by the city at night; LOW and MEDIUM draw a cheap layer from the same field.
- Cloud shadows drift over the streets, roofs and walls in the chase view too (the same clouds as overhead).
- Aerial perspective in the chase view: the haze thins with height (towers rise out of it), is warmer and brighter looking into the sun, and takes the sky's own colour, so the far city melts into the sky; the street view's haze is unchanged.
- Sun glare in the chase view: veiling glare (MEDIUM up), light shafts through the gaps between buildings and trees and a restrained lens flare (HIGH, ULTRA), all fading when the sun is blocked, behind a cloud, off screen or down.
- The environment map is the same sky without the disc, tuned to light the city as before (within a few per cent).
- Internals: aerial-haze3d.js (fog chunks; `cityHaze*` uniforms shared by every fog-enabled material), lighting3d-sky-dome.js, clouds3d-sky.js, postfx3d-sun.js; the composite moved to postfx3d-composite.js (pure move). Console: `cloudLayer().view.sky`.
