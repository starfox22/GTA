# Pedestrians see cars coming, and a second pass is lethal

- A pedestrian now reacts to a car only if they could notice it. Facing it they mostly step aside or leap clear (a few freeze, jump back or go the wrong way); with their back to it most never know and are hit unaware, with no cry; a few hear it at the last moment (more with the horn, a siren or a screech, fewer in heavy traffic or with earbuds) and turn too late. People on a phone, texting or chatting are slower, the old and the drunk too, and a car that appears close or fast gives nobody time. Someone who has just looked both ways at a kerb sees all round for a few seconds; once crossing they look where they walk.
- Run over someone who survived the first hit and is still on the ground and it hurts for real: a crawl maims (they stay down, groaning), 30 km/h or a heavy vehicle kills them within a second or two, with a splash and streaks on the road, a pool once they are dead, tyre prints, a stain on the car, a crunch, and a groan first if they were conscious. One kill, one reward, as for any death; the same car cannot repeat it while still over them.
