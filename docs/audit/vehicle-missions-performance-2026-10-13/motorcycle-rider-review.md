# Motorcycle rider contacts

The baseline GPU screenshots show the CB750 rider's wrists near the mirrors, above the rubber grips, and the cruiser rider's arms reaching backward toward the pillion. These are authored contact errors. The CB750 packed hint was `[0.2699, 1.2579, 0.4431]` metres; the cruiser hint `[-0.9707, 1.1817, 0.5949]` selected rear grab bars. The converter's outermost/highest steering-vertex heuristic can select mirrors and rear handles instead of the handgrips.

The corrected CB750 contacts are `[0.28578, 1.06470, -0.32901]` and `[0.29343, 1.05870, 0.32271]`. Their independent cylinder fits use 22/46 unique dark rubber vertices from the actual packed `trim`/`steer.trim` meshes; radii are 14.37/14.44mm and RMS radial residuals 1.20/1.06mm. The cruiser contacts are `[0.17248, 1.20221, -0.39207]` and `[0.15636, 1.23054, 0.28577]`: two separate welded rubber components (84/80 indexed vertices, 52/58 unique positions), fitted radii 19.67/19.31mm and RMS residuals 2.77/2.81mm. Source display lean and a slightly displaced centerline make the contacts asymmetric. A mirrored average misses a bar.

Root added the measured pairs to the packed header and reproducible converter overrides. `motorbike-assets3d.js` scales each pair once and caches the inverse fixed fork-pivot matrix. `crowd3d-special.js` consumes these raw body-space contacts without an extra wrist offset, then transforms them through that inverse and the current `m.steer.matrixWorld`. Existing single-grip seats keep their compensated wrist convention. Seat position, feet, rider lean, arm lengths and the drive-by hand override retain their existing behavior. No additional meshes or draws are introduced; the rider uses existing vector scratch storage.

`tools/tests/motorcycle-rider-fit.mjs` runs the actual packed builders, rider queue, pose target function, crowd shoulder transforms and two-bone arm IK with real Three.js. All **102 assertions pass** on the corrected actual pack. Rest, both steering directions, bank, body pitch, yaw, wheelie, player/ambient rider branches, seat/peg attachment and legacy single-grip behavior are covered. The target error is zero; bone-length error is below `9e-15`. Running the same assertions with the baseline `bde9ee4` rider source and corrected pack isolates the rider transform and produces **34 failures**, including steering contacts displaced by up to 1.529 world units. This comparison does not substitute the corrected geometry metadata for a full original-build graphics capture; the separate baseline GPU screenshots provide that visual evidence.

The existing finite arm reach is preserved. The primitive 1.8m player's CB750 right wrist is about 4mm short at straight bars and about 60mm short at the tested +0.46rad full steering lock. The host checks the exact physical clamp instead of stretching bones or guessing a pose offset. Cruiser and KR500 contacts remain within this tested player's reach. Final AFTER screenshots are owned by the root/Moto capture task.

KR500 keeps its previous authored grip hint `[0.27, 1.21, 0.35]`; its mirrored legacy contacts now follow steering. Independent geometry measurements found circular 15.15mm shaft rings, but the negative-side shaft/rubber surfaces cross the `trim`/`steer.trim` partition and include vertices displaced from the fitted ring axis. A corrected full rubber-grip midpoint is not proven, so no guessed paired contact was applied. Exact follow-up evidence is recorded in `motorcycle-rider-grip-measurements.json` and `motorcycle-kr-grip-followup.json`.

Read-only frame audit also confirmed terrain pitch/roll were added after `finishCrowd3D` drained riders and occupants. Root moved the existing slope transforms before that crowd pass, so wrists, saddle and occupants use the final terrain-aligned vehicle matrices. The host executes the actual frame span with real Three.js matrices: current rider/occupant copies match the final tilted chassis, while the baseline frame produces two attachment failures (motorbike and car). Boats, aircraft and invisible-model exclusions retain their original behavior.

Evidence: `motorcycle-rider-host-after.json`, `motorcycle-rider-host-before-source.json`, `motorcycle-rider-host-before-frame.json`, `motorcycle-rider-grip-measurements.json`, `motorcycle-cruiser-grips.json`. The archived component/measurement scripts decode the packed coordinates and texture atlas; they are audit tools and do not rewrite assets.

The CB750 left grip has 22 unique sampled rubber positions in `steer.trim`; 13 of them also occur in body `trim` at crossing control/cable triangles. The corrected contact is on the moving steering surface, but any visible fragment separation at full steering should be reviewed as a mesh partition issue. No geometry payload was changed by the grip metadata correction.
